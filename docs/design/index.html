Three concepts for the social-tunnel tray app

Same product, same words, same states. What changes is the tray mark and its motion, the kind of dropdown, and how setup and settings are laid out. All three are native macOS in feel, with frosted glass over the desktop. Press Play under each mark to watch connected, lost, reconnecting, connected, blocked.

A · Portal

A tunnel mouth in the menu bar. It follows SF Symbols habits: a slash means off, an exclamation mark means it needs you. The dropdown is a real system menu, so the frosted glass, the keyboard and VoiceOver come from the system.

Compare

The state to compare first is Disconnected: it is where the three differ most.

A · PortalB · RouteC · Beacon
DirectionQuiet native. SF Symbols habits, system menu.Shows the path. The route is the whole interface.Read at a glance. One silhouette per state.
Tray mark
MotionFill, drain, sweepDraw, pull back, walking dotsPop, ripple, turning arc
DropdownNative menu. One line per row.Popover with the hop route.Panel of tiles plus menu rows.
Setup and settingsThree-step assistant, tabbed settingsRoute as stepper, route on top of tabsOne page of cards, one page of rows
Strengths
  • Least to build: the native tray menu works on macOS, Windows and Linux.
  • Vibrancy, keyboard and screen reader support come from the system, not from us.
  • The arch is the most ownable mark of the three.
  • Shows which hop failed without opening anything else.
  • Room for the full fix hint and a copy button.
  • One component, the route, is tray mark, status strip and setup stepper.
  • Easiest to read at a glance: each state has its own silhouette.
  • The most visible transitions, and still calm: one beat, then still.
  • Room for cause, fix and a button without drawing a diagram.
Risks
  • Rows cannot wrap. doctor’s fix strings run to about 500 characters today, so each needs a short form of about 44.
  • All four states share one outline; the difference sits inside a 9 px doorway.
  • Native menus dim disabled rows more than this mock shows. Info rows may need to be enabled rows with no action.
  • A custom popover window: position, focus, dismissal, keyboard and VoiceOver are ours to build.
  • Linux trays only open menus, so a native menu fallback is still needed.
  • Least legible mark at 16 px: the state lives in the thin middle of the glyph.
  • Closest of the three to a dashboard.
  • Least distinctive mark: rings and dots are common in the menu bar, and a solid dot can read as a recording light.
  • Same custom-window cost as B, and the same Linux fallback.
  • Ink buttons ignore the system accent colour.

Recommendation

One concept to build, one thing to borrow.

Build C · Beacon. The brief puts the tray mark first, and C is the only set where every state has its own silhouette at 16 px. Its changes are also the easiest to notice from the corner of the eye, and they stop moving once the state settles.

Borrow from A: the native menu. Linux needs it anyway, and it makes a cheaper first release. The rows and words match the panel in C, so the mark from C can ship with the menu from A first, and the frosted panel can follow.

Keep the route from B for Settings. The hop strip is the best explanation of a failure, but it turns the dropdown into a small dashboard. It fits better as the Connection group in the settings window.

Main risk: the ring is the least distinctive mark. Use the arch from A as the app icon (Dock, installer, About) so the product still has its own face.
